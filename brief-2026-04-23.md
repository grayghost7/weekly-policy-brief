## 3. AI & Law Intersections

- **US Bill Mandates On-Device Age Verification** — Federal proposal to move age gating to the OS layer; implicates Section 230, First Amendment access doctrine, and biometric data law.
- **Illinois Operating System Age Verification Law** — State-level preemption questions and a likely test case for whether OS-level ID checks survive constitutional scrutiny.
- **Brussels age-checking app broken in 2 minutes** — Raises regulatory questions about whether compliance with EU age assurance rules can rest on demonstrably insecure tech.
- **"Call it identity verification, not age verification"** — Frames the policy debate: if systems collect government ID or biometrics, existing data protection law (GDPR, state biometric statutes like BIPA) should apply more stringently.
- **US Visa Applicants Ordered to Make Social Media Public** — First Amendment (for domestic-facing speech chilling), APA challenges, and international data-protection frictions.
- **France ID agency breach** — Fuel for arguments that centralized identity systems — including those proposed for age verification — are structurally risky.
- **San José Flock Safety lawsuit** — A key test case on whether ALPR mass-surveillance networks violate the Fourth Amendment and state constitutional privacy protections.
- **FaceWatch retail facial recognition (UK)** — Private-sector biometric use without due process; relevant to UK DPA/GDPR and emerging US state biometric laws.
- **NY 3D printer/CNC censorware proposal** — Novel collision of firearm regulation, compelled-speech doctrine, and software liability.
- **Google auto-scanning photos** — Revives CSAM-scanning debates (cf. Apple's 2021 retreat) and Fourth Amendment "private search" doctrine when cloud providers act quasi-governmentally.
- **House extension of surveillance powers to April 30** — FISA Section 702 reauthorization politics; ongoing unresolved questions about warrant requirements for US-person queries.
- **Meta's mandatory employee AI training program** — Employment law and emerging questions about worker consent to behavioral biometric data collection.
- **Anna's Archive $322M judgment** — Precedent on large-scale scraping liability under copyright — directly relevant to AI training data legality.
- **Anti-AI violence / Altman attack suspect** — Foreshadows executive-protection law, threats statutes, and platform duty-of-care questions around radicalization content.
- **Palantir manifesto + national service push** — Raises questions about government-contractor ideological speech and procurement law.
- **AI-generated "Emily Hart" MAGA influencer** — Implicates FTC deception authority, emerging state AI-disclosure laws (CA AB 2655/2839), and election-integrity statutes.
- **Rep. Roy's MAMDANI Act** — Denaturalization based on political ideology raises First Amendment and *Afroyim v. Rusk* concerns.
- **Leaked SCOTUS memos** — Implicates judicial ethics reform debates and congressional authority over Court transparency.

## 4. Emerging Questions

1. **Can "age verification" mandates survive First Amendment scrutiny when they functionally operate as identity verification?** After *NetChoice v. Paxton* and with OS-level mandates now proposed, what is the least-restrictive-means analysis when every implementation so far collects ID or biometrics?
2. **What legal framework governs ALPR and facial-recognition networks operated by private vendors but functionally integrated with police (Flock, FaceWatch)?** Are these state actors for Fourth Amendment purposes, and do existing biometric privacy statutes reach them?
3. **How should copyright and tort law treat the asymmetry between AI companies training on scraped data and shadow libraries doing the same?** The Anna's Archive judgment sits uneasily alongside ongoing AI-training fair-use litigation.
4. **What is the constitutional ceiling on ideologically-based denaturalization or immigration enforcement** (MAMDANI Act, visa social-media disclosure), given *Afroyim* and evolving compelled-disclosure doctrine?
